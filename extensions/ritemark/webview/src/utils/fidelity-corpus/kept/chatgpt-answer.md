## Short answer

Yes. A quarterly review works best when it is short, has a fixed agenda, and ends with decisions written down.

---

## How to run it

1. **Send the agenda a week ahead.** Include the three numbers everyone should look at.
2. **Start with the decisions from last time.** Were they done? If not, why?
3. **Keep the discussion to one hour.** Park anything that needs a deeper look.

### What to bring

- The pipeline report
- Last quarter's targets
- One page per team, no more

---

## A simple template

| Section | Owner | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Decisions from last time | Chair | 10 min |
| Numbers | Finance | 15 min |
| Team updates | Each lead | 25 min |
| New decisions | Chair | 10 min |

## Example follow-up email

```text
Subject: Q3 review - decisions

Hi all,

Thanks for today. Three decisions:
1. Hire one more support person.
2. Move the launch to 14 November.
3. Review pricing in January.
```

**Tip:** write the decisions in the meeting, not after it.
