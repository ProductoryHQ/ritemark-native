The bet: a transcript is just another document. Ritemark is already good at documents; it only needs to learn how to make one out of audio.

Insights come from the existing AI sidebar acting on the open document — no separate insights engine.

What’s new

  • One activity-bar item — “Transcribe”, with a progress badge while a job runs
  • Job queue + library of finished transcripts, with the engine used shown on every row
  • Engine selector at the top: the privacy choice is visible before you start, not buried in Settings
  • Transcript writer — speaker headings, timestamps, front-matter — producing a real .md in the workspace

What it reuses

  • The Ritemark editor is the transcript viewer — no new editor surface
  • The AI sidebar is the insights feature — summaries, decisions, memos
  • Export to PDF/DOCX and (later) Cloud sharing work on day one
  • Existing Whisper stack for the on-device engine

What you give up

  • No audio playback tied to the text — you can’t click a line to hear it
  • Speaker renaming is find-and-replace, not a first-class action
  • Risk it reads as a converter rather than a product — the app is a queue, and the queue is empty most of the time
  • Correction work happens in the editor, where a wrong edit is indistinguishable from a real one
