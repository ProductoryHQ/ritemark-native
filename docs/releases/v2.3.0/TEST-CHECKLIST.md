# Ritemark 2.3.0 Test Checklist

> **Started on 16 November 2026** at the close-out of Sprints 159 and 160, before 2.3's release plan and candidate exist. It holds what those two sprints couldn't run, for Jarmo to test on the release candidate. The candidate's table, the Gate 1 and Gate 2 rows, Sprint 158's Not run rows and the sign-off are added when the candidate is built, as in [2.2's checklist](../v2.2.0/TEST-CHECKLIST.md).

Where the rows come from: [Sprint 159's close-out](../../development/releases/v2.3.0/sprint-159-decks-training/sprint-plan.md#close-out) and [Sprint 160's](../../development/releases/v2.3.0/sprint-160-ai-ready-day-one/sprint-plan.md#close-out).

- **Your own profile** is the main test. Rows marked **Fresh** run in a test instance with its own throwaway folders and AI homes, started as in [2.2's How to start](../v2.2.0/TEST-CHECKLIST.md#how-to-start).
- **What a row needs** is in bold at its end: **Projector** = a projector or a second display, **Clicker** = a presentation clicker, **Cloudflare** = Jarmo's Cloudflare test account, **Phone** = a real phone, **Windows** = a Windows PC, **AI** = a real signed-in AI (named), **Key** = a real API key (named), **Fresh** = a fresh profile. No mark means the candidate alone is enough.
- **The source** is in small text at the end: the sprint, its PR and the PR's number.
- Tick a row only when it passes. A row that fails gets its issue number beside it. A row not run says why and stays unticked.

## Decks (flag on: `"ritemark.features.decks": true` in user settings)

Add the line to your user settings, then open a deck; the [demo project](../../development/sprints/sprint-157-decks/demo-project/README.md) has several. The Slide library comes after Tasks, and New has Deck.

1. [ ] **The wall on a projector.** Connect the projector and press Present. The slide fills the projector, full screen at 16:9 with dark bands, and nothing of Ritemark shows: no title row, tabs, project name or desk edge. **Projector** <sub>159 PRs 5 and 6, #731, #736</sub>
2. [ ] **The presenter view on the laptop.** With the projector connected, it comes on by itself, and Present's menu names the display ("Slides on <name>, notes here"). It shows the slide now and the next one, the notes (A− and A+ change their size), the row of slides, the clock and Next, in Ritemark Dark, with "Claude and notices wait until you stop." Esc closes both windows, and the deck is back on the slide you ended on. While presenting, change the words of the slide on the wall in the deck: the presenter view's Now stays as the wall shows it, and the row shows the change; press Next and then Back, and both show it. **Projector** <sub>159 PR 6, #736, #740</sub>
3. [ ] **Swap displays.** The wall and the presenter view change displays, each still full screen. Swap again, and they are back as they were. **Projector** <sub>159 PR 6, #736</sub>
4. [ ] **A clicker.** Its forward and back buttons move the wall, whichever of the two windows has the focus. **Projector, Clicker** <sub>159 PRs 5 and 6, #731, #736</sub>
5. [ ] **Publish a deck.** Press Publish on the deck's bar. The sheet's line under its title reads "People open it in their browser, on a computer or a phone.", and What goes and What stays count this deck's slides, prompts, speaker notes and skipped slides. Publish it. The chip reads Published; See online opens the page, and Copy link copies its address. Unpublish, then switch Card | Borderless and press Publish straight after: the page has the look you chose, and the chip reads Published. **Cloudflare** <sub>159 PR 4, #737, #740</sub>
6. [ ] **The page on a phone.** Open the address on your phone. The slide stays at the top and the text scrolls under it; Copy on a prompt says Copied (or "Tap Copy in your phone's menu"); an exercise's ticks are still there after a reload; Contents works. No speaker notes and no skipped slide are on the page. **Phone, Cloudflare** <sub>159 PR 3, #729</sub>
7. [ ] **Safari and Firefox.** The same page on a computer, in each: the slide on the left with arrows under it, the text scrolling on the right, and the slide following the text. **Cloudflare** <sub>159 PR 3, #729</sub>
8. [ ] **Update the page.** Change a slide's words: the chip reads Published · changed. Update the page, and the page shows the change. Then change only a speaker note: the chip stays Published. **Cloudflare** <sub>159 PR 4, #737</sub>
9. [ ] **Unpublish.** From the chip's menu. The address no longer shows the page, and the bar shows Publish again. **Cloudflare** <sub>159 PR 4, #737</sub>
10. [ ] **Sprint 157's checklist on the candidate.** Run [Sprint 157's rundev checklist](../../development/sprints/sprint-157-decks/qa/rundev-checklist.md) with the flag on. It ran on the Decks branch, never on main after the merge. <sub>159 PR 2, #735</sub>
11. [ ] **A fresh profile with the flag already on.** Write the line into a fresh profile's user settings before its first start. The Slide library is in the places row from the first frame, after Tasks, and nothing in the row moves. **Fresh** <sub>159 PR 1, #733</sub>
12. [ ] **The bundle's cost.** With the flag off, a long Markdown document opens as fast as in 2.2.0: time it from a cold start on both. Every editor now carries Decks, about 258 KB more. <sub>159 PR 2, #735</sub>
13. [ ] **Decks on Windows.** On a Windows PC: a deck opens as a deck, New → Deck opens an Untitled deck with its start card and its copy row works (rows 14 and 16), Present works on one display, and Publish and the page work as in rows 5–9. The second display on Windows is its own short task. **Windows** <sub>159</sub>
14. [ ] **New → Deck opens the deck.** In Documents, choose a folder, then New → Deck. A tab "Untitled" opens at once in that folder, with no window or pick before it: one title slide reading "The deck's title", selected so typing replaces it. Under the handout's first part is the card "Start this deck". New → Deck again makes "Untitled 2". With the flag off, New has no Deck. <sub>159, New → Deck, #749</sub>
15. [ ] **Ask Claude to draft it.** On a new deck's card, write what the deck is for, and press Draft (Enter does the same). The AI column opens if it was folded, your words are in it as a message with the deck attached, and Claude writes the slides and the handout into the deck. In a test instance with no AI signed in, the card has only the copy row; in a project with no other deck as well, there is no card. **AI: Claude, Fresh** <sub>159, New → Deck, #749</sub>
16. [ ] **Or copy an earlier deck.** On a new deck's card, choose one of the three newest decks, or one from All decks…. The deck has that deck's slides, theme and Card | Borderless, with its pictures showing; the tab reads "<deck name> (copy)", and the bar says "This deck is now a copy of <deck name>." Then on another new deck, type a word in the handout, add a slide, or press ×: the card goes, and it isn't there when the deck is opened again. <sub>159, New → Deck, #749</sub>
17. [ ] **The handout reads as a Text document.** Open a deck from the demo project. The handout has the Text editor's type, headings and dark code blocks; between slides there is one thin line from the dotted divider to the right edge, with the slide's number at the right of the part's first line (in the accent for the current slide). No grey section band, no "SECTION 1", no bar beside the current part, no Copy button of the deck's own on a prompt (the code block's own copy icon shows on hover). The handout takes the wider share; drag the divider, close and reopen the deck: the share you chose stays. In /, Divider reads "Divider · a new slide", and the other items are a Text document's. <sub>159, the handout as text, #750</sub>
18. [ ] **An older deck writes nothing until you edit it.** Copy the demo project's "AI assistants and agents.deck.md" aside, open the original as a deck, and wait a minute without touching it: each part shows its slide's title as a heading (a section's larger), the tab shows no unsaved dot, and the file is byte for byte the copy (compare them). Type one letter in the handout: the file now has a `##` (or `#`) heading under each slide block and `handout: text` in its front matter, and the slide blocks themselves are unchanged. Delete one part's heading, close the deck and open it again: the heading stays deleted. Publish it: the page shows the same headings, and Contents lists the slides' titles. <sub>159, the handout as text, #750</sub>
19. [ ] **A heading and a title never change each other.** Change a part's heading in the handout: the slide's title doesn't change, and a library slide's copy doesn't show "Changed in this deck". Change the slide's title on the stage: the heading stays. Under a narrow slide, "Changed in this deck; …" has a line of its own, with its buttons under it. <sub>159, the handout as text, #750</sub>
20. [ ] **A line starts a new slide.** In a part's text, on an empty line, type `***` and Enter (or `---`, or Divider from /): a new slide comes in after the current one in the strip, and the caret is in the new part's empty heading. Undo takes it back in one step. Type `***` over an existing heading's line: the new slide takes that heading as its title. On a line with nothing under it, type a heading in the new part's empty heading and go on to the text: the slide's title is that heading; change the heading afterwards and the title stays. A line inside a list or a quote stays a line. Backspace at the start of the new part never removes the slide (Delete in the strip does, with Undo). **Not built in this cut, so not tested:** pasting text with lines in it makes no slides (the lines stay lines), and Backspace at a heading doesn't join a slide to the one before. <sub>159, the handout as text, #750</sub>
